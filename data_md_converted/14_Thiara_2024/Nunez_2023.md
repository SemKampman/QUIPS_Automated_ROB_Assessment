#### **Abstract**

**Purpose** Hemorrhagic stroke (HS) is a devastating complication during extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) but markers of risk stratifcation during COVID-19 are unknown. Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) is a readily available biomarker of cell injury and permeability. We sought to determine whether an elevated LDH before ECMO placement is related to the occurrence of HS during ECMO for COVID-19.

**Methods** Adult patients with COVID-19 requiring ECMO between March 2020 and February 2022 were included. LDH values prior to ECMO placement were captured. Patients were categorized into high (>750 U/L) or low (≤750 U/L) LDH groups. Multivariable regression modeling was used to determine the association between LDH and HS during ECMO.

**Results** There were 520 patients that underwent ECMO placement in 17 centers and 384 had an available LDH. Of whom, 122 (32%) had a high LDH. The overall incidence of HS was 10.9%, and patients with high LDH had a higher incidence of HS than those with low LDH level (17% vs 8%, *p*=0.007). At 100 days, the probability of a HS was 40% in the high LDH group and 23% in those with a low LDH, *p*=0.002. After adjustment for clinical covariates, high LDH remained associated with subsequent HS (aHR: 2.64, 95% CI 1.39–4.92). Findings were similar when restricting to patients supported by venovenous ECMO only.

**Conclusion** Elevated LDH prior to ECMO cannulation is associated with a higher incidence of HS during device support. LDH can risk stratify cases for impending cerebral bleeding during ECMO.

# **Introduction**

Extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) has had a critical role during refractory respiratory or cardiopulmonary failure from COVID-19 [1–4]. The majority of cases are indicated for acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS)-related respiratory failure with venovenous extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (VV-ECMO). Despite advances in medical treatment for COVID-19, mortality for patients requiring ECMO remains elevated, ranging from 30 to 55% [2, 5–8]. To improve outcomes, it is essential to identify baseline factors and biomarkers that may assist in risk stratifcation. Among factors associated with mortality, hemorrhagic stroke (HS) represents the single most important complication associated with death [3, 7, 9–18]. Moreover,

small studies have indicated that the incidence of HS is elevated during ECMO for COVID-19 [19]. Early identifcation of patients at risk for HS could serve as a potential measure to ameliorate the incidence of this devastating complication and improve morbidity and mortality.

Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) is a readily available biomarker of global tissue and cellular injury and is elevated during severe COVID-19 infection, associated with worse outcomes among these patients [20–22]. LDH has been used as a prognostication marker for neurological complications in non-ECMO settings, as it has been positively linked with increased endothelial damage and vascular permeability [21, 23–28]. However, its role in risk stratifcation for HS in COVID-19 cases treated with ECMO remains uncertain. Using a multicenter cohort of patients, we sought to determine whether an elevated LDH before cannulation is related to the occurrence of HS during ECMO support for COVID-19 infection.

# **Methods and Materials**

## **Patient Selection**

All adult patients undergoing ECMO support for respiratory and/or cardiac support with COVID-19 infection between March 2020 and February 2022 from participating centers were included in this retrospective cohort analysis. Patients with no available data on LDH were excluded (Fig. 1). Data source was a voluntary registry populated by 17 participating centers located in diferent regions in the United States. Data entry, training and validation procedures of this registry have been published [29]. This study was approved by the institutional review board at montefore medical center.

## **Exposure and Outcomes**

The exposure of interest was serum LDH prior to ECMO cannulation. The study cohort was categorized into patients with high (>750 U/L) or low (≤750 U/L) LDH. This threshold was derived by applying receiver operating characteristics curve analysis to identify a cut-point with optimal sensitivity and specifcity for HS (sensitivity 0.61, specifcity 0.60). The primary outcome was occurrence of HS, evidenced via brain imaging using head computed tomography as clinically indicated based on clinical judgement by the treated physician..

## **Statistical Analysis**

Categorical variables are expressed as counts and percentages and were evaluated using the chi-squared test. Continuous variables are presented as means and standard deviations (SD) or medians and interquartile ranges (IQR)

as appropriate and compared between groups using Student's *T*-test or Mann–Whitney test according to the distribution of the variable. Kaplan Meier analyses was used to estimate and compare the probability of HS during ECMO between patients with a high or low LDH. Patients were censored at 100 days after ECMO initiation. Censoring was done for ischemic stroke, weaning of ECMO, device upgrade, or death. Multivariable Cox proportional hazard models were used to determine the risk of HS for patients with a high LDH in comparison those with a low LDH. Adjustment was performed for covariates present in>90% of patients, previously known to be associated with HS and those with a univariable associated with LDH groups (*P* < 0.2). A subgroup analysis was performed for patients cannulated for venovenous ECMO. Adjusted hazard ratios (aHR) and 95% confdence intervals (CI) are reported. All statistical tests were two-sided and *p*<0.05 was considered for statistical signifcance. All analyses were performed using Stata 14.0 (StataCorp LLC, Texas).

## **Hemorrhagic Stroke and LDH**

Twenty-one out of 122 (17%) patients with a high LDH experienced HS in comparison to 21 out of 262 (8%) with a low LDH (*p*=0.007). Patients that experienced a HS, had a higher LDH prior to ECMO placement (737 vs. 589 U/L, *p*=0.021, Fig. 2).

At 100 days after ECMO initiation, the probability of a HS was 40% in the high LDH group and 23% in those with a low LDH, *p*=0.002 (Fig. 3A).

After adjustment for age, sex, prone positioning, ECMO duration, and pre-ECMO serum creatinine, patients in the high LDH group retained a greater likelihood of an eventual HS in comparison to those with low LDH (aHR, 2.40, 95% CI 1.27–4.51, *p*=0.007). Findings remained consistent in separate multivariable models with adjustment for age, sex, pre-ECMO pH and lactic acid (aHR, 2.24, 95% CI 1.15–4.38, *p*=0.018), age, sex, pre-ECMO platelets and ferritin (aHR, 3.30, 95% CI 1.65–6.59, *p*=0.001, Fig. 3B). In subgroup analysis of patients cannulated for venovenous ECMO, those with a high LDH retained a greater risk of an eventual HS, (aHR 2.40, 95% CI [1.28, 4.47], *p*=0.007), which remained similar in adjusted models (Fig. 4).

# **Discussion**

We report a multicenter retrospective study examining the occurrence of HS and its relation to LDH during ECMO support for severe COVID-19 infection. The major fndings of this study are as follows: 1) The occurrence of HS during ECMO for COVID-19 is elevated at 10.9%, 2) patients experiencing HS are at a substantially greater risk of death and 3) an elevated LDH over 750 U/L prior to ECMO placement is associated with an almost three-fold increase in developing HS during device support. This association between LDH and HS persisted after adjusting for confounders. Additionally, fndings were preserved after limiting our analysis to patients cannulated for VV-ECMO.

HS remains a relatively common complication during ECMO with a remarkably strong association with mortality [9, 10, 19]. Recent studies show that the risk of HS can be substantially higher among COVID-19 patients compared to non-COVID-19 patients undergoing ECMO support [19, 30]. Seelinger et al. conducted a retrospective multicenter study among COVID-19 patients on ECMO, noting an HS incidence of 20%. Their study revealed a six-fold increased risk of HS compared to similar non-COVID-19 patients receiving ECMO for viral infection [19]. Numerous studies have elucidated risk factors for HS during ECMO in non-COVID-19 populations. Some of these factors include pre-ECMO hyperoxemia, higher infammatory markers, thrombocytopenia, worse renal function, vasopressor use, higher lactate, and rapid decreases in PaCO2 after ECMO initiation [10, 15, 17, 31–34]. We also noted that patients who experienced a HS, at baseline had worse renal function and acidosis. Reverberation of these risk factors with HS during refractory COVID-19 infection, indicates that a greater severity of underlying illness and infammation are culprit in triggering HS. Importantly, we identify LDH as a readily available biomarker that may independently capture this infammatory milieu and risk of eventual cerebral bleeding. The magnitude of LDH elevation in our cohort may refect a more severe infammatory state induced by COVID-19 infection, further demonstrated by the higher levels of other infammatory markers, including ferritin and C-reactive protein.

LDH is an intracellular enzyme that gets released with cellular injury due to inflammatory conditions such as COVID-19 [26, 35]. Its use in prognostication has been established in many critical conditions including patients with COVID-19 [20, 25, 35–37]. However, evidence supporting its role in predicting HS remains uncertain. LDH levels can be elevated in cerebrospinal fuid of patients with stroke [38]. Its elevation is associated with worse outcomes in patients with both ischemic stroke and intracranial hemorrhage [24, 25, 39]. Chu et.al determined that LDH levels predict hematoma expansion in patients with HS using a proteomic analysis [26], a fnding that was later confrmed by Wang et al. in a much larger study of about 360 patients [27]. Despite these studies relating LDH to risk stratifcation for eventual neurologic injury during ECMO in patients with COVID-19 has not been established previously.

The mechanisms by which LDH may risk stratify patients for HS could be related to the underlying infammatory response that accompanies severe COVID-19 cases requiring ECMO. The infammatory cascade can result in progressive endothelial damage of brain vessels leading to the disruption of the blood–brain barrier [26, 27]. In COVID-19 specifcally, it is known that the infammatory response is exacerbated and is a major driver of the subsequent complications. Endotheliopathy involving intracranial vessels together with anticoagulation and ECMO-induced coagulopathy may constitute a microenvironment leading to an increased risk for HS [19, 40–42]. Our fndings indicate that LDH can serve as a prognostic marker prior to ECMO cannulation, useful for risk stratifcation, that could help clinicians' decision-making for optimal ECMO candidacy and expectations before cannulation. Additional prospective studies are needed to validate our fndings and determine whether patients higher pre-ECMO LDH would incur a lower burden of HS with minimization of anticoagulation during ECMO during COVID-19 and beyond.

# **Conclusion**

An elevated LDH >750 U/L prior to ECMO initiation is associated with a higher risk of HS during device support in patients with COVID-19. Further prospective studies are warranted to confrm these fndings and to examine whether reduction in anticoagulation in these patients can reduce the burden of brain hemorrhage.
