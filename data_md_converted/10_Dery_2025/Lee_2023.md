# **Proteomic discovery of prognostic protein biomarkers for persisting problems after mild traumatic brain injury**

**Min‑Yong Lee1,2,10, Minsoo Son3,4,10, Hyun Haeng Lee1,5, Min‑Gu Kang1 , Seo JungYun1 , HanGil Seo1,6, Youngsoo Kim3,7,8,10**\* **& Byung‑Mo Oh1,2,6,9,10**\*

**Some individuals with mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI), also known as concussion, have neuropsychiatric and physical problems that last longer than a few months. Symptoms following mTBI are not only impacted by the kind and severity of the injury but also by the post-injury experience and the individual's responses to it, making the persistence of mTBI particularly difcult to predict. We aimed to identify prognostic blood-based protein biomarkers predicting 6-month outcomes, in light of the clinical course after the injury, in a longitudinal mTBI cohort (N= 42). Among 420 target proteins quantifed by multiple-reaction monitoring-mass spectrometry assays of blood samples, 31, 43, and 15 proteins were signifcantly associated with the poor recovery of neuropsychological symptoms at < 72 h, 1 week, and 1 month after the injury, respectively. Sequential associations among clinical assessments (depressive symptoms and cognitive function) afecting the 6-month outcomes were evaluated. Then, candidate biomarker proteins indirectly afecting the outcome via neuropsychological symptoms were identifed. Using the identifed proteins, prognostic models that can predict the 6-month outcome of mTBI were developed. These protein biomarkers established in the context of the clinical course of mTBI may have potential for clinical application.**

Traumatic brain injury (TBI) is a signifcant public health issue with approximately 69 million new cases worldwide each year and causing nearly 61,000 deaths in the United States[1,](#page-11-0) [2](#page-11-1) . TBI can cause short- or long-term issues that may afect a person's life, such as the inability to work or resume social activities[3](#page-11-2) . Even with mild TBI (mTBI), commonly known as concussion, which accounts for 70–90% of all TBI cases, the morbidity is substantia[l4](#page-11-3) . mTBI can result in acute symptoms, including headache, dizziness, irritability, fatigue, cognitive difculty (e.g., attention and memory), and sleep disturbances[5](#page-11-4) . In most patients with mTBI, these symptoms resolve within days to weeks afer the injury; however, some patients develop persistent physical and neuropsychiatric symptoms called post-concussion syndrome (PCS)[5](#page-11-4) . Reportedly, up to 40% of the patients with mTBI have symptoms persisting for more than 6 months[6,](#page-11-5) [7](#page-11-6) , and many patients with PCS experience impairments in daily life and a decrease in quality of life for an extended period, impeding their return to work or schoo[l3](#page-11-2), [8](#page-11-7) .

Considering the huge personal and societal burden of mTBI, it is important to identify patients who will have sequelae of their injury and in whom early interventions would be helpful in a timely manner. Tus, a prognostic tool for outcome prediction afer mTBI can aid surveillance eforts and the provision of rehabilitation services efectively and cost-efciently[9](#page-11-8) . Although a few models for the prediction of functional outcome afer mTBI have been developed using multiple predictive factors, including demographic, socioeconomic, pre-injury and

Tere has been a growing interest in the potential of biochemical biomarkers to predict mTBI prognosi[s14,](#page-11-12) [15](#page-11-13). However, although several biomarkers, such as S100 calcium-binding protein B (S100B), glial fbrillary acidic protein (GFAP), and ubiquitin carboxy-terminal hydrolase L1 (UCH-L1), have been studie[d16,](#page-11-14) [17](#page-11-15), no biomarker has been found that accurately predicts the outcome afer mTBI. In previous studies, these biomarkers have been explored using a hypothesis-driven approach[18](#page-11-16), with limited success. Terefore, data-driven approaches such as microRNA arrays or proteomics that can complement this traditional approach have been suggeste[d18](#page-11-16). Particularly, mass spectrometry-based proteomics can precisely quantify hundreds of proteins from a complex mixture of samples, enabling the identifcation of more useful biomarkers[19.](#page-11-17) However, a limitation of mTBI biomarker research is that the majority of studies have reported values of biomarkers taken at a single time point afer injury[15](#page-11-13). In mTBI, studying changes in biomarkers over a short time window is suboptimal because patients present to a clinic or hospital at various stages afer injury[20](#page-11-18). Tus, it is important to perform longitudinal studies over time afer mTB[I15.](#page-11-13) A longitudinal study could provide a useful background to identify candidate prognostic biomarkers of mTBI.

Terefore, we aimed to identify candidate prognostic biomarkers of recovery from mTBI using mass spectrometry-based proteomics and develop a predictive model for prognosis at 6 months afer injury in a prospective longitudinal cohort of patients with mTBI.

#### **Results**

#### **Patient characteristics**

A total of 48 patients were enrolled during the 6-month follow-up, and 42 patients were analyzed in the study (six patients were excluded because of a missed follow-up beyond 1 week afer injury [n=2] or failure to collect serum samples [n = 4]). Most of the 42 participants were previously healthy, except for 6 participants with a history of hypertension or diabetes. Neuropsychological assessments, such as the Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II), Korean-Montreal Cognitive Assessment (K-MoCA), and Frontal Assessment Battery (FAB) scores, and the serum samples at four time points afer injury (<72 h, 1 week, 1 month, and 3 months) from the 42 patients were used to identify the candidate prognostic biomarkers for recovery from mTBI.

For prognostic classifcation, the patients were classifed into the poor recovery (PR) or good recovery (GR) groups based on the outcomes (assessed using the Rivermead Post-concussion Symptoms Questionnaire [RPCQ] and Glasgow Outcome Scale-Extended [GOSE] at 6 months). Te RPCQ and GOSE measure the subjective distress associated with diverse physical, emotional, and cognitive symptoms and the level of daily function, respectively. Overall, three of the 42 patients who had follow-up information only for 3 months (n=1) or were not followed up at 3 or 6 months (n=2) were excluded from the prognostic classifcation. Te serum samples from 22 patients in the GR group and 17 patients in the PR group were used for developing a prognostic model (Table [1\)](#page-2-0). Te PR and GR prognostic groups difered signifcantly with regard to RPCQ scores at 1 week (Student's t-test, *P*=0.0016, respectively), GOSE scores at 3 months (Student's t-test, *P*=0.0026, respectively), and BDI-II scores at 3 months and 6 months (Student's t-test, *P*=0.0022 and *P*=2.28e−5, respectively). Te 1- and 3-month K-MoCA and FAB scores were not signifcantly diferent between the PR and GR groups.

#### **Association of longitudinal proteomic alterations in the serum with the 6‑month outcomes**

To select analytic targets, we compiled a total of 3130 candidates of target proteins from three comprehensive TBI animal model studies using RNAseq techniques with well-known TBI biomarker lists (Supplementary Table S1). Among them, we selected 763 peptides representing 528 proteins as detectable targets in plasma. Out of 528 proteins, 420 proteins were used for data processing, according to interference-free and skewness in overall samples (Supplementary Methods and Supplementary Table S2).

To select a candidate prognostic biomarker of mTBI, linear regression analysis was performed to verify the association between 6-month outcomes (RPCQ and GOSE scores) and plasma levels of 420 qualifed proteins at the four time points. Proteins associated with a higher risk for poor prognosis (positive correlation with the RPCQ scores or negative correlation with the GOSE score) were classifed as high-risk proteins. In contrast, proteins associated with a lower risk of poor prognosis were classifed as low-risk proteins. In the regression analysis of the RPCQ score at 6 months, 31 proteins (high-risk protein/low-risk protein: 15/16) within 72 h, 43 proteins (31/12) at 1 week, 15 proteins (8/7) at 1 month, and 26 proteins (9/17) at 3 months showed a signifcant association. Additionally, 36 proteins (high-risk protein/low-risk protein: 8/28) within 72 h, 27 proteins (16/11) at 1 week, 16 proteins (9/7) at 1 month, and 27 proteins (22/5) at 3 months were signifcantly associated with the GOSE score at 6 months (Fig. [1a](#page-3-0)).

Te characteristics of the selected proteins are summarized as follows: (1) within 72 h, low-risk proteins were more enriched than high-risk proteins by 3.5-fold relative to the GOSE score; (2) at 1 week, high-risk proteins were more enriched than low-risk proteins, relative to both RPCQ (2.6-fold) and GOSE scores (1.5-fold); (3) at 3 months, low-risk proteins for the RPCQ score were more enriched than high-risk proteins by 1.9-fold, whereas high-risk proteins associated with the GOSE score were more enriched than low-risk proteins by 4.4-fold; and (4) the percentage of common proteins associated with the RPCQ and GOSE scores decreased sequentially over time, implying that more diferent biological events afected the RPCQ and GOSE scores at later time points (Fig. [1](#page-3-0)b). Te list of proteins associated with changes in the RPCQ and GOSE scores is presented in Fig. [1](#page-3-0)c.

#### **Predicting outcomes using protein biomarker panels**

To assess the prognosis of patients with mTBI (GR vs. PR) using plasma protein levels in an objective manner, logistic regression analysis was performed at each time point (39 patients). Univariate analysis of the proteins indirectly afecting prognostic outcomes showed an association with PR (Wald test, *P*<0.1), with 12 of 18 proteins (66.7%) within 72 h, 12 of 16 proteins (75.0%) at 1 week, all 4 proteins (100.0%) at 1 month, and 6 of 13 proteins (46.2%) at 3 months (Supplementary Table S7). Multivariate logistic regression analysis with backward selection by 10 iterations of threefold cross-validation showed that microsomal glutathione S-transferase 1, neuroflament heavy chain, and retinoic acid receptor responder 2 levels at 1 week; activated leukocyte cell adhesion molecule, macrophage migration inhibitory factor, and syntaxin-binding protein 1 at 1 month; and insulin-like growth factor-binding protein 5 (IGFBP5), interferon regulatory factor 8, and lipoprotein lipase at 3 months were independent prognostic factors at 6 months (Supplementary Table S7). We could not fnd independent variables within 72 h because of the small sample size (N=14). Tis prognostic stratifcation scheme classifed the PR group with approximately 80.0% accuracy and area under the curve and could assess the biological events associated with prognosis (Table [2](#page-6-0) and Supplementary Fig. 3). Te longitudinal expression of protein variables in the models is shown in Supplementary Fig. 4.

#### **Discussion**

In this prospective study, we identifed candidate prognostic biomarkers using proteomics and developed prognostic models at 6 months afer injury in a longitudinal cohort of 42 patients with mTBI. First, proteins signifcantly associated with the 6-month outcome were selected among the proteins quantifed by the highly quantitative multiple reaction monitoring-mass spectrometry (MRM-MS) assays (Fig. [1\)](#page-3-0). Next, we identifed sequential associations among clinical assessments afecting the 6-month outcome of mTBI (Fig. [2](#page-4-0)). Ten, we identifed candidate prognostic biomarker proteins that indirectly afected the 6-month outcome via neuropsychological symptoms (BDI-II or K-MoCA and FAB paths) (Fig. [3\)](#page-5-0). Using the candidate biomarker proteins, the prognostic models for each phase that can predict the 6-month outcome of mTBI were developed (Table [2\)](#page-6-0).

By analyzing the biological terms of these prognostic candidate biomarker proteins, we could determine implications for the pathophysiology of mTBI. Te sequence from the events that occurred within 72 h afer injury to the prognostic outcomes at 6 months is summarized as follows (Fig. [4](#page-7-0)): (1) acute events caused infammation during the frst week, followed by depressive symptoms, which worsened the RPCQ and GOSE scores at 6 months; (2) at 1 week, the secretion of proteins from axons and neuroflament to plasma was associated with a poor RPCQ score via impaired cognitive function tested by the K-MoCA, whereas the mitochondrial proteins related to infammation were associated with a poor GOSE score via cognitive decline tested by the FAB; (3) the efect of depression on the RPCQ score varied depending on the myelin sheath degradation at 1 month and alterations in the hormone levels and glucose homeostasis at 3 months; and (4) the efect of cognitive decline (FAB score) on the GOSE score varied depending on neuronal damage at 1 month and alterations in lipoproteins and LPS-IFNγ proteins at 3 months.

At 1 week, the main biological term associated with the outcome was infammation. Infammation has an important role in the pathophysiology of secondary brain injury afer TBI[21](#page-11-19). Since post-traumatic infammatory response plays an important role in neurodegeneration, corticosteroids were used afer TBI until the early 21th century, when the CRASH trial was published[22.](#page-11-20) Although the CRASH trial showed that corticosteroids should not be used routinely afer TBI, this does not negate the role of infammation in TBI. Te neuroinfammation following mTBI has been hypothesized to be associated with post-concussive symptoms[23,](#page-11-21) [24](#page-11-22). An interesting study reported that initially elevated C-reactive protein levels might be an independent predictor of persistent PCS in patients with mTB[I25.](#page-11-23) Similar results that infammation may infuence the outcome, mediating depressive symptoms and cognitive function, were found in this study. However, further investigation of the role of infammation in the sequelae following mTBI is required.

Axon and neuroflament proteins in the plasma at 1 week showed a correlation with RPCQ at 6 months via cognitive decline at 3 months in this study. Te subsequent axonal damage is one of the most common pathological mechanisms of long-term dysfunction in mTB[I21.](#page-11-19) A few studies have reported that cognitive dysfunction can correlate with the extent of microstructural white matter damag[e21,](#page-11-19) [23](#page-11-21). Tus, axon-related proteins, such as neuroflament light (NFL), spectrin N-terminal fragment (SNTF), and A-tau, have been extensively studied as biomarkers of TBI[26](#page-11-24). ALCAM is known to play a role in cell adhesion, axon growth, axonal pathfnding, and neuronal migration and diferentiation[27.](#page-11-25) Tere is a paucity of studies on ALCAM as a TBI biomarker; however, it may have potential as a candidate biomarker in TBI. Mitochondrion was also identifed in the subacute phase (1 week) as a biological term that can infuence the outcome of mTBI via cognitive decline. Mitochondrial dysfunction has been reported in animal models and humans and is considered to be an acute response afer TBI[28](#page-11-26), [29.](#page-11-27) Te primary function of mitochondria is the production of adenosine triphosphate (ATP) and reactive oxygen specie[s30.](#page-11-28) Tus, mitochondria dysfunction can exacerbate the cellular energy crisis and aggravate cell damage[28,](#page-11-26) [31](#page-11-29). Tis energy crisis can impact post-concussive symptoms and increase the vulnerability to second impact syndrome in mTBI patient[s31,](#page-11-29) [32](#page-11-30).

At 1 month, the efect of depression on the 6-month RPCQ score varied depending on the degradation of the myelin sheath. Myelin surrounds and protects axons in the central nervous system (CNS). Afer TBI, myelin damage can result from axon injury, neuronal cell death, or secondary damage that causes oligodendrocyte loss with subsequent demyelination of intact axons[33](#page-11-31). Although the role of myelin injury in the pathophysiology of mTBI remains poorly understood, it is known that myelin damage contributes to white matter injury along with axonal injur[y34,](#page-11-32) [35](#page-12-0); thus, myelin can be associated with cognitive functio[n36.](#page-12-1) However, the present study difers in that myelin showed an association with depressive symptoms. Although the association between depressive symptoms and decreased white matter integrity in multiple brain areas afer mTBI was reported in a previous study[37](#page-12-2), it needs further investigation.

Moreover, microglial activation terms, such as neuronal damage, were associated with 6-month GOSE via cognitive decline at 1 month. Microglia are the immune cells of the CNS that have roles in brain infammation through their interaction with white matter[38,](#page-12-3) [39](#page-12-4). Microglial activation has recently been of increasing interest because they have been recognized as a key regulator of degeneration and regeneration of the white matte[r39](#page-12-4). As white matter injury is known to be a major contributor to the impairments associated with mTBI, microglial activation has been studied as a biomarker and a potential target of therapeutics in mTBI[39,](#page-12-4) [40.](#page-12-5) Although the results of the present study might be supportive of this, there is still much to be revealed.

At 3 months, alterations in lipoproteins and LPS-IFNγ were associated with the 6-month outcome via cognitive decline. Among lipoproteins, lipoprotein receptor protein 1 (LRP1) was a candidate biomarker identifed in this study. LRP1, a regulator of blood–brain barrier (BBB) integrity, may have roles in dementia progressio[n41,](#page-12-6) [42.](#page-12-7) TBI-induced BBB dysfunction has been reported in previous studies[42](#page-12-7). However, there is a paucity of studies examining the role of LRP1 in TBI. Meanwhile, IFN-γ may play a signifcant role in the pathophysiology of TBI as a neuroinfammatory mediator[43](#page-12-8). A study reported that IFN-γ level in mTBI patient group was signifcantly increased compared with that in the community control group at 1 year afer injury, suggesting an activation of the innate immune system up to 1 year post-injury[44](#page-12-9). Although our study also showed similar results, further research is needed.

In addition, glucose homeostasis showed a correlation with RPCQ at 6 months, mediating depressive symptoms at 3 months. In this study, the candidate biomarker protein related to glucose homeostasis was IGFBP5, which can exert both stimulatory and inhibitory efects on IGF-1 signalin[g45](#page-12-10). Although IGF-1 exhibits both neuroprotective and neuroregenerative efects afer TB[I46](#page-12-11), further studies are needed on the potential of IGFBP5 as a mTBI biomarker.

Because of the heterogeneity of mTBI, it is difcult to predict the prognosis of the patients. Until recently, several studies have attempted to predict the prognosis using multiple factors[7,](#page-11-6) [47](#page-12-12), [48](#page-12-13). Tese models were mainly based on clinical factors, such as admission characteristics, post-injury symptoms, and psychological symptoms. However, in an external validation study using the Collaborative European NeuroTrauma Efectiveness Research in Traumatic Brain Injury (CENTER-TBI) data, none of these predictive models showed good calibration and discrimination capabilities in patients with mTB[I7](#page-11-6) .

Blood-based biomarkers have been proposed as novel predictors of mTBI prognosis. Commonly studied mTBI prognostic biomarkers, such as GFAP, S100B, neuron-specifc enolase, and UCH-L1, have shown poor discriminating ability[18](#page-11-16). Ghrelin, NFL, SNTF, and A-tau have been studied as potential biomarkers for the prognosis of mTBI[26](#page-11-24), [49](#page-12-14)[–51](#page-12-15). For example, a recent study that was conducted to predict recovery afer sports-related concussion in professional fat-track jockeys using blood biomarkers, such as GFAP, NFL, and tau, suggested that cognitive testing and blood biomarkers may be potential objective measures to assist in the monitoring of concussion recover[y52.](#page-12-16) However, the relation of ghrelin, NFL, SNTF, and A-tau is limited to axonal injury, which is one of the most common pathological mechanisms of long-term dysfunction in mTB[I21.](#page-11-19) Te pathophysiology of mTBI is highly complex; therefore, it is necessary to discover various candidate biomarkers that can comprehensively cover the pathophysiology.

Unlike previous studies of mTBI biomarkers that used a hypothesis-driven approach, we utilized proteomics, a data-driven approach, to discover novel prognostic candidate biomarkers. Although the hypothesisdriven approach is less likely to yield false-positive results, it is limited by current understanding and the time it requires[18](#page-11-16). In data-driven methods such as proteomics, a large number of candidate biomarkers can be discovered quickly because candidate biomarkers can be screened without regard to the pathophysiology, although it is more likely to yield false-positive results[53](#page-12-17). To reduce false-positive results of proteomics, serial mediation and moderation analyses were performed in this study. Although cross-sectional approaches to mediation are possible to generate biased estimates of longitudinal parameters in the special case of complete mediation[54](#page-12-18), serial mediation analysis is powerful and widely used to fnd mediators between variables. We investigated a causal chain linking the mediators between clinical symptoms and time progression and then selected the proteins that mediated these causal linking chains and infuenced the 6-month outcome. Tus, the selected proteins might be more reliable as mTBI prognostic biomarkers.

Tis study identifed candidate mTBI prognostic biomarkers at multiple time points from the early to the chronic phase afer injury, whereas previous studies have mostly identifed biomarkers only at a single time point. A biomarker that is available in the acute phase of injury is not appropriate in the chronic phase. For example, the level of GFAP in peripheral blood reaches a peak at 20 h afer trauma but is detected at a very low concentration afer 72 [h17](#page-11-15). Biomarkers such as GFAP can be used for diagnosis or prediction of the outcome in the acute phase of injury, but it is difcult to use them afer 3 days of injury. Because patients with mTBI may not visit the hospital in the acute stage of the injury alone, the candidate prognostic biomarkers at various time points identifed in this study are expected to be clinically useful.

Te present study used both the RPCQ and GOSE as outcomes for developing prognostic models of mTBI. Although there is an overlap between both measures, there is also a diference in that RPCQ refects patients' discomfort, while GOSE refects the overall function. Terefore, in previous studies, post-concussive symptoms measured by RPCQ or GOSE at 6 months afer injury have been used as the outcome of mTB[I7](#page-11-6) . However, both measures have limitations as outcome variables. Although RPCQ is employed most ofen in measuring post-concussive symptom[s55,](#page-12-19) there is no guidance for assessing PCS using RPCQ. GOSE is widely employed as a primary outcome measure in TBI studies, but its utility as an outcome measure in patients with mTBI is controversial because GOSE is not sensitive enough to fnd diferent health problems despite good functioning[7](#page-11-6) . Terefore, to complement these limitations, we used both outcomes of mTBI for developing prognostic models in this study.

Tis study has some limitations. Tis study only includes a single time point (3 months) in the early chronic phase. It would be better to conduct blood sample collection and clinical assessments between 1 and 3 months and beyond the 3-month period. Te validity of the serial use of assessment tools has not yet been validated. Measurements at some time points were missed in a few participants because of the time of their enrollment. In particular, we could not fnd the independent variables in the logistic regression due to a lower number of blood samples within 72 h afer trauma and could fnd only single variables. To enhance patient care in the emergency department (ED), biomarkers that can be applied within 72 h should be identifed in further studies. For dichotomization of the RPCQ at 6 months, we matched 16 post-concussive symptoms of the RPCQ to 8 symptoms of the ICD-10 criteria. Although it is not suitable for diagnosis of PCS because the RPCQ is a questionnaire, several studies have employed ICD-10 matchin[g47,](#page-12-12) [56](#page-12-20). Another limitation is that the use of mass spectrometry data requires sophisticated sofware and expertise, and data interpretation can be complex and prone to errors. In addition, there may be a selection bias toward more severe patients because this study was conducted at a tertiary hospital. A future multicenter study would ensure the representativeness of the sample group and a larger sample size.

In conclusion, we identifed prognostic candidate biomarkers using proteomics in light of clinical course afer mTBI. Although our results should be externally validated for generalizability, the established protein biomarkers in the clinical course of mTBI may have potential for clinical application.

#### **Methods**

#### **Ethics statements**

Te ethics committee of Seoul National University Hospital, Korea, approved the study (1603-147-751 and 1805-079-947). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. All methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations.

#### **Study design and population**

Tis prospective longitudinal cohort study enrolled patients with head trauma at the ED or outpatient clinic of Seoul National University Hospital from January 2018 to January 2021. Te inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age ≥ 18 years, (2) patients presenting within 1 month afer head trauma, and (3) diagnosis of mTBI. Te diagnosis of mTBI was based on the World Health Organization diagnostic criteria:

"Operational mTBI criteria for clinical identifcation include (a) 1 or more of the following: confusion or disorientation, loss of consciousness for 30 min or less, post-traumatic amnesia for less than 24 h, and/or other transient neurological abnormalities such as focal signs, seizure, and intracranial lesion not requiring surgery; (b) Glasgow Coma Scale score of 13–15 afer 30 min post-injury or later upon presentation for health care. Tese manifestations of mTBI must not be caused by drugs, alcohol, caused by other injuries or treatment for other injuries (e.g., systemic injuries, facial injuries, or intubation), caused by other problems (e.g., psychological trauma, language barrier, or coexisting medical conditions), or caused by penetrating craniocerebral injury["57](#page-12-21).

Te exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Injury Severity Score (ISS)≥16; (2) current infectious disease; (3) history of brain disorders (e.g., stroke, TBI, brain tumor, or Parkinson's disease); (4) history of drug abuse; and (5) current pregnancy. Te ISS is a medical score used to assess trauma severity based on the worst injury of the six body systems. Each injury in every body region is assigned to an abbreviated injury scale (AIS) on a six-point ordinal scale (1: minor, 6: maximal). To calculate the ISS (ranging from 3 to 75), we selected the highest AIS in each of the three most severely injured body regions, squared each AIS, and added the three squared number[s58](#page-12-22).

#### **Study procedure**

When a patient with head trauma was identifed in the ED or outpatient clinic, physicians of the research team evaluated patient eligibility for the study. Enrolled patients were carefully interviewed, and their medical records were reviewed. Data including demographics (age, sex, height, and weight), injury-related information (the time of injury, mechanism, initial Glasgow Coma Scale scores within 72 h, loss of consciousness [LOC], and posttraumatic amnesia), brain computed tomography fndings, medical history, and history of substance abuse were collected from medical records and interviews with the patient or patient's family.

Afer enrollment, the patients visited the clinic at multiple time points afer injury (<72 h, at 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, and 6 months). Blood samples were collected within 72 h, at 1 week, 1 month, and 3 months afer injury. Since we planned to recruit patients within 1 month afer head trauma, blood samples within 72 h or at 1 week could not be obtained depending on the time of enrollment. Blood samples were collected into two 10-mL ethylenediamine tetraacetic acid tubes and three serum separation transport tubes of 15 mL for the serum, centrifuged, aliquoted, labeled, and stored in a freezer at -80 °C until they were transported for analysis at each visit.

Clinical assessments were evaluated at 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, and 6 months afer injury. If the patient was enrolled 1 week afer injury, clinical assessments at 1 week could not be evaluated.

#### **Clinical assessments**

We evaluated neuropsychological symptoms, including depressive symptoms and cognitive dysfunction. Depressive symptoms were assessed using the BDI-II at 1, 3, and 6 months afer injury. Te BDI-II is a widely used 21-item self-report inventory that evaluates the severity of depression. Each item is rated on a 4-point scale ranging from 0 to 3. Te maximum total score is 6[359.](#page-12-23) Cognitive function was assessed using the K-MoCA and FAB at 1 and 3 months. To improve the accuracy of assessing neuropsychological symptoms, several cognitive measures have been created and introduced. Nevertheless, as a result of early post-concussion symptoms, including intense fatigue, sleep disorders, and anxiety, experienced by many patients, we have decided to use the K-MoCA and FAB tests. Tese tests are less time-consuming, preventing excessively lengthy assessment times. Te K-MoCA is a 30-point test that explores the following: short-term memory, visuospatial ability, executive function, attention, concentration, working memory, language, and orientatio[n60,](#page-12-24) [61.](#page-12-25) Te MoCA has been shown to have higher sensitivity for memory testing than the MMSE in mild cognitive impairment[62](#page-12-26). Although MoCA has not had its validity confrmed as a screening tool for cognitive impairment in mTBI, numerous studies of mTBI have used the MoCA for measuring cognitive function[63](#page-12-27)[–66](#page-12-28). Te FAB consists of six subtests evaluating conceptualization, mental fexibility, motor programming, sensitivity to interference, inhibitory control, and environmental autonomy. Te total FAB score ranges from 0 to 18 and takes approximately 10 min[67.](#page-12-29) Te FAB was designed to assess frontal lobe function including executive functioning. Patients who experience an mTBI show signifcantly worse cognitive performance on general cognitive ability, naming, memory, and executive functioning afer the injury[68](#page-12-30). Given that the FAB better assesses executive function and has also been employed in previous studies[68](#page-12-30), we selected it as a complementary tool to K-MoCA.

Post-concussive symptoms were evaluated using the RPCQ at 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, and 6 months afer injury, where the patient is asked to weigh the severity of 16 post-concussive symptoms compared with premorbid levels, using values from 0 to 4. Te total RPCQ score ranges from 0 to 6[469.](#page-12-31)

Overall function was evaluated using the GOSE at 1 month, 3 months, and 6 months afer injury. Te GOSE consists of eight stages: 1, dead; 2, vegetative; 3, lower severe disability; 4, upper severe disability; 5, lower moderate disability; 6, upper moderate disability; 7, lower good recovery; and 8, upper good recovery. Te GOSE score was assessed using questions from structured GOSE interviews[70](#page-12-32).

#### **Outcome**

In this study, the main outcome measures were the RPCQ and GOSE at 6 months afer injury. A lower RPCQ score and a higher GOSE score suggest better outcomes. When developing a predictive model with a binary outcome variable, RPCQ and GOSE scores were dichotomized into poor and good outcomes. For dichotomization of the RPCQ score, we used the International Classifcation of Diseases, Tenth Edition (ICD-10) criteria for PCS. Te ICD-10 criteria for PCS were used because the ICD-11 criteria had not been released yet when we planned and started the study in January 2018.

Patients with a history of head trauma, usually sufciently severe to result in LOC, were diagnosed with PCS if they met at least three of the following criteria: (1) headache, (2) dizziness, (3) fatigue, (4) irritability, (5) memory impairment, (6) difculty concentrating and performing mental tasks, (7) insomnia, and (8) reduced tolerance to alcohol, stress, or emotional excitement[57](#page-12-21). Te RPCQ cannot be used to diagnose PCS. In this study, we matched 16 post-concussive symptoms of the RPCQ to 8 symptoms of the ICD-10 criteria (see details in Supplementary Methods). If the value for each symptom was 2 (a mild problem) or more, the patient was considered to have the matched sign of the ICD-10 criteria. Terefore, if the patients' RPCQ score met the requirements for PCS based on the ICD-10 criteria, they were considered to have a poor RPCQ score. Regarding GOSE, a score < 8 was regarded as a poor GOSE score in this study. Patients with poor RPCQ and GOSE scores at 6 months afer injury were classifed as having PR and other patients were classifed as having GR.

#### **Proteomic analysis**

In total, 117 plasma samples were prepared in block-randomized batches with respect to age, sex, and prognostic recovery group (PR vs. GR) using the "psych" package (version 1.9.12) in R (version 4.0.5; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Te six highly abundant plasma proteins were depleted using a Multiple Afnity Removal System Human-6 column (MARS Hu-6, 4.6 mm×100 mm; Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Proteins (100 μg) were digested to tryptic peptides with RapiGest bufer, and digested peptides were desalted using Oasis® HLB cartridges (Waters Corp., Milford, MA, USA). A volume of a 10-µL crude stable isotopelabeled standard (SIS) peptide mixture (JPT Peptide Technology, Acton, MA, USA) was spiked into each 90-µL peptide sample for MRM-MS analysis, a highly selective and reproducible proteomics techniqu[e71](#page-12-33) (see details in Supplementary Methods).

Te plasma samples were analyzed using an Agilent 6490 triple quadrupole mass spectrometer (Agilent) with a Jetstream electrospray source coupled to a 1260 Infnity HPLC system (Agilent) (see details in Supplementary Methods). Protein levels were measured using MRM-MS, and raw MRM-MS data were processed using Skyline (MacCoss Lab; University of Washington, Seattle, WA) to compute the peak area of the transitions (see details of analytical target selection in Supplementary Methods)[72.](#page-12-34) Each sample was analyzed in single-replicate and block-randomized batches. Targets with interference signals were excluded from the automated detection of an inaccurate and imprecise transitions algorithm (see details in Supplementary Methods)[73.](#page-12-35) Te quantity of spiked SIS peptides for individual analyses was matched with each endogenous target level in the pooled matrix sample. Te peak area ratios (peak areas of endogenous peptides normalized to those of their corresponding SIS peptides) were used to compare the relative abundance of candidate peptides between the samples. Te log10-transformed peak area ratios were used for data processing. Proteins with high skewness (>1.5 or<− 1.5) were excluded from data processing.

#### **Statistical analysis**

Te skewness of the log-transformed protein levels was assessed using the "e1071" package (version 1.7-3) in R (version 4.0.5). Student's t-test, χ<sup>2</sup> test, Pearson's correlation analysis, and cubic splines function were performed for the longitudinal expression of proteins using base packages in R. Linear regression and logistic regression analyses were performed using the "caret" packages (version 6.0-86) in R. Serial mediation and moderation analyses were performed using the "processR" package (version 0.2.6) and "lavaan" package (version 0.6-9) in R (see details in Supplementary Methods). Gene ontology functional annotation of proteins was performed using DAVID Bioinformatics Resources (version 6.8; <https://david.ncifcrf.gov/>).
